# A.R.C. UI review — 29 August 2026

Audience: children aged 7–10, on a landscape-locked iPhone or iPad
(`ios/App/App/Info.plist:63-71`) plus the web fallback. Every judgement below is
made against that audience, at the three viewports the project's own harness
uses: 812×375, 1024×768, 1280×800.

Method: source reading, plus measurement. Text widths and line heights were
measured in Chrome against the self-hosted Nunito faces the game actually ships
(`public/fonts/fonts.css`); the HTML overlay screens were loaded at real device
sizes and measured in the DOM. Nothing here is inferred from a screenshot of the
running game — WebGL does not initialise in the review harness, and per
`.claude/TRAPS.md` click-testing through `ErrorOverlay` is not trustworthy. Where
a finding is reasoned rather than observed, it says so, and the unverified
material is quarantined in the last section.

---

## The short version

The art is good and the game logic underneath is sound. What is wrong is almost
entirely **layering and ownership of screen space** — three systems (the painted
canvas, the left rail, the HUD/nav chrome) each lay themselves out as though they
own the full viewport, and then draw on top of one another. The result reads as a
prototype because it literally is one in places: `LeftRailView` was introduced to
pull interactive UI off the painted scenery, the old UI was deleted with
`PROTOTYPE:` comments left behind (`CorridorView.ts:270,376,383`,
`HUDView.ts:151`), and nothing was re-laid-out around the 280px column the rail
now occupies.

The three complaints map cleanly onto causes:

| Complaint | Cause |
|---|---|
| Overlapping elements | An opaque 280px rail at depth 50 over a full-width canvas at depth 0; plus four decorations positioned against a sprite box that is half the sprite's real size |
| Poor legibility | Animal name pills at 1.50–2.10:1 contrast for six of eight species; 14px button labels against the project's own 18px floor |
| Not kid-friendly | The exit button is off-screen on two ceremony screens; five percentage bars where two mean the opposite of the other three |

Two of these are not polish. On a landscape iPhone a child who opens the Paths
screen cannot get out of it, and on both iPhone and iPad a child cannot decline
an adoption. Those come first.

## Findings, ranked by impact on a child's ability to play

Each is marked **NEW** or **KNOWN**. The existing notes
(`docs/landscape-ux-2026-08-27.md`, `docs/audit-2026-08-22.md`) closed every
font-size and tap-target failure the harness can see, and the harness measures
sizes, spacing and margins — **not overlap**, and only at scene start, across
eight scenes. It therefore never opens the animal popup and never opens an HTML
overlay. Everything below in those two areas is new by construction.

---

### 1. The way out of the Paths screen is off the bottom of the screen — NEW · blocking

`apps/game/public/admin/paths.html` (mounted by
`src/game-overlay/InGameOverlay.ts:65` as a full-viewport iframe).

Measured at 812×375 with `?embed=1`, as the game mounts it:

```
.welcome        scrollHeight 384  clientHeight 375   overflow-y: hidden
.link-btn "← Back to Luna"   top 365   bottom 411
```

The only exit control starts 10px below the fold and ends 36px past it, and the
panel cannot be scrolled.

The cause is one attribute. Every overlay carries a design-preview device frame,
and the markup hardcodes `data-size="ipad-p"` (768×1024, portrait). The
`?embed=1` branch adds `body.embed` and the `html.iframed` rules stretch the
device to `100%/100%` — but nothing ever changes `data-size`. The stylesheet's
own overflow escape hatch is written as
`.device[data-size="desktop"] …, .device[data-size="ipad-l"] … { overflow-y: auto !important }`,
so on a landscape-locked device it never applies. The authors already knew the
content overflows in landscape — the comment above that rule says so — and the
mitigation is keyed to two values the game never sets.

This is systemic: **19 of 23 shipped overlay screens default to
`data-size="ipad-p"`, and none sets it in embed mode.**

Why it matters at 7–10: a child cannot reason "the button must be below, let me
try dragging". They tap where they can see, and when nothing works they close the
app. This screen is the gate to adoption and rewilding — the game's payoff.

Fix, in order of cost:
1. In `InGameOverlay.mountInGame`, append `&size=ipad-l` (or read the viewport)
   and have the embed branch set `device.dataset.size` from it. One line each
   side, fixes all 19 screens.
2. Better: drop the device-frame simulation entirely in embed mode — set
   `html.iframed .device { width:100%; height:100%; overflow-y:auto }` — so the
   panel scrolls whatever the viewport.
3. Best: pin the exit control to the bottom of the viewport
   (`position: sticky; bottom: 0`) so it can never be the thing that scrolls
   away.

### 2. A child cannot decline an adoption — NEW · blocking

`apps/game/public/admin/adoption.html`, same mount path.

| Viewport | `.adopt` scroll/client | `← Not yet, let me hug her first` |
|---|---|---|
| 812×375 | 383 / 375 | off-screen (15 elements below the fold, including the whole "Thank-you gifts" panel) |
| 1024×768 | 783 / 768 | top 748, bottom 793 — 25px cut, unscrollable |

The opt-out from the adoption ceremony is unreachable on **both** device classes.
The only remaining path is to complete the adoption, which is irreversible and
removes the animal from the shelter.

For this audience that is the worst possible failure mode: a child who has bonded
with an animal, is not ready to let it go, and is given a screen with no way back
learns that the game took her animal away. Same fixes as finding 1; the sticky
footer matters most here.

### 3. The left rail covers the game — NEW · high

`src/game-views/LeftRailView.ts:30,51-62`; `src/scenes/GameScene.ts:208`.

The rail is `RAIL_WIDTH = 280` px, full height from `y = 110` to the bottom of the
screen, painted `0xfef9ef` at **0.96 alpha** — effectively opaque — and mounted at
`setDepth(50)`. Everything it covers is at the default depth 0: the painted
canvas (`gameContainer`), the nav bar (`navContainer`), the HUD (`uiContainer`)
and the animal details popup.

280px is **34.5% of a landscape iPhone's width** and 27% of an iPad's. Nothing
below it was re-laid-out.

**3a. 36% of the hand-authored sprite positions are behind it.**
`public/data/room-anchors.json` holds 100 anchors, each an x-fraction of the full
scene width (`GameScene.ts:756`, `CorridorView.ts:323`). 36 of them fall at
x < 0.345 (phone), 27 at x < 0.273 (iPad). Among them:

```
/corridor/parrot/arriving[0]     x=0.102   →   83px on phone,  104px on iPad
/corridor/bat/arriving[0]        x=0.152   →  123px          ,  156px
/corridor/cat/arriving[0]        x=0.300   →  244px          ,  307px (clear on iPad)
/corridor/__decor/sign-snake[0]  x=0.043   →   35px          ,   44px
/corridor/__decor/sign-fox[0]    x=0.202   →  164px          ,  207px
```

The last two are **door signs** — the control a child taps to enter a species
room. On both device classes the snake and fox doors are painted behind an opaque
cream panel.

**3b. The procedural door slots are behind it too.** `CorridorView.ts:96-104`
places seven doors at fixed x-fractions. At 0.14 (bat) the 140px sign spans
44–184 on a phone and 73–213 on an iPad: entirely covered. At 0.27 (fox) it is
~90% covered on a phone. Even the cat door at 0.40 loses its left edge on a
phone.

**3c. The "Home" nav tab is entirely underneath it on a phone.** With
`NavBarView.ts:90-96` the bar is 464px wide, so at 812px width `barX = 174` and
the first tab's hit rect is x 191–265, y 287–351. The rail's rectangle is
x 0–280, y 110–375. The tab is inside it, and the rail is drawn on top.

**3d. …and an interactive rail control lands on it.** With one arrival waiting,
the rail's "Welcome" button (`LeftRailView.ts:395-400`) is centred at x 140,
y 320, sized 224×47 → x 28–252, y 296–343. That overlaps the Home tab's hit rect
across **61 × 47 px**. Phaser sorts its input list by depth, so the rail (50)
should take the tap ahead of the nav bar (0): a child aiming at Home welcomes an
animal instead. *(Reasoned from depth ordering, not click-tested — see the
unverified section.)*

**3e. The details popup slides under it.** `GameScene.ts:1006` renders the popup
into `this.gameContainer`, i.e. depth 0. Its 320px panel is clamped to
`anchor.x` (`AnimalDetailsPopup.ts:168`), so tapping an animal on the left of a
room puts most of its own information panel behind the rail.

**3f. The garden has it too.** `GardenView.ts:285,440` lay pets out from
`width * 0.15` and `width * 0.2` — 122px and 162px on a phone, both inside the
rail.

Why it matters at 7–10: this age reads the screen as a picture, not as a stack of
layers. An animal that is 40% hidden behind a cream rectangle is not "behind a
panel" to a child — it is broken. And an invisible-but-functional Home button is
worse than a missing one: the child stops believing taps do anything.

Fix, in order:
1. **Reserve the rail's width in the canvas layout.** Every view already takes
   `const { width } = scene.scale`. Introduce a single `getStageBounds(scene)`
   that returns `{ x: railW, y: hudH, w: width - railW, h: … }` and have
   `CorridorView`, `RoomView` and `GardenView` lay out inside it. Anchor
   fractions then resolve against the stage, not the screen — which is what the
   anchor editor was drawing against before the rail existed.
2. **Or** make the rail an overlay that can be pulled away: a 56px tab on the
   left edge that slides the panel in, closed by default. That gets the animals
   their full canvas back and costs one tween.
3. Either way, **re-author or re-map the 36 anchors**, and give the anchor editor
   (`public/admin/anchors.html`) the same rail gutter so the next batch is
   authored against what the child sees.

### 4. The details popup is taller than a landscape phone — NEW · high

`src/game-views/AnimalDetailsPopup.ts:136`:

```ts
const panelH = 44 + 44 + speechH + 5 * 18 + actionRows * 46 + 28;
```

For a shelter animal that is sick, walkable, outside and needs a coat,
`actionRows` reaches 5 and `panelH` = 466px. The placement code
(`:155-167`) then clamps the panel between a 40px top margin and an 80px bottom
margin; on a 375px-tall screen that resolves to `panelTop = 40`,
`panelBottom = 506`. **131px of the panel, and its last two or three action
buttons, are below the bottom edge of the screen.** There is no scrolling: the
scene's drag/wheel handlers move `gameContainer` and `maxScrollY` is only ever
set to 0 (`CorridorView.ts:395` is the sole caller of `setMaxScrollY`).

Three action rows is already enough to push 39px off the bottom.

**And one button is not budgeted for at all.** The `extraActionRows` sum
(`:125-133`) has a term for every action *except* the Paths button added at
`:386-394`. Whenever an animal reaches bond ≥ 50, the panel is 46px shorter than
its own content and `💫 What will {name} become?` hangs off the bottom edge of
the cream panel, over the dimmed scene.

That button is the entry point to the entire adoption/rewilding endgame, and it
renders half-outside its own container.

Fix:
- Add `(!isPet && animal.bondLevel >= PATHS_UNLOCK_BOND && !!callbacks.onOpenPaths ? 1 : 0)`
  to `extraActionRows`. One line, removes the overflow.
- Replace the arithmetic height with a measured one: lay the rows out, then size
  the panel from the cursor. The current expression already mis-budgets the
  header (it allows 44px; the content starts at `panelTop + 60`).
- Cap `panelH` at `height - 32` and put the action list in a scrolling column, or
  — better, see the redesign below — stop putting eight buttons in one popup.

### 5. Every label that decorates an animal is drawn *inside* the animal — NEW (consequence) / KNOWN (cause) · high

Cause is known: `src/ui/sprites.ts:112` multiplies the fit scale by 2, so a
sprite asked for a 100px box renders at 160px (audit finding 13/11, recorded as
"worth checking against the layouts"). Here is what it does to the layouts.

`RoomView.ts` positions four things relative to `size` — the box it *asked* for,
not the box it *got*:

| Element | Line | Placed at | Sprite's real edge | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name pill | `:190-197` | `y + size/2 + 14` = y+64 | y+80 | pill sits 16px inside the animal's feet |
| Bond bar | `:207` | `y + size/2 + 32` = y+82 | y+80 | just clears |
| Status chips | `:386-387` | `x + size/2 - 4` = x+46, y−44…y+12 | x+80 | all three chips on the animal's body |
| Sibling icon | `:221` | `x − size/2 + 6` = x−44 | x−80 | on the animal's body |

So the animal's own name is printed across its legs, and its status badges are
printed across its chest. That is the single biggest reason the screens read as
unfinished, and it is one multiplication.

`CorridorView.ts:365` has the same bug in a different form: it passes
`size: drawW` (90) to the popup anchor while the sprite is 148px wide, so the
popup's "pop above the sprite" maths is wrong by ~29px in each direction and the
panel overlaps the animal it describes.

Fix: delete the `* 2` and multiply the ~12 call sites' requested boxes instead
(`RoomView`, `GardenView`, `CorridorView`, `WalkScene`, `PlayScene`, the picker
views), so requested size means rendered size. Then place decorations from
`sprite.getBounds()` rather than from `size` — `CorridorView.ts:357` already does
exactly this for the safe-margin clamp and documents why.

### 6. Six of eight animal names are illegible — NEW · high

`RoomView.ts:190-197` draws the name in **white bold 16px on the species colour**
at 0.85 alpha. Contrast against `SPECIES_COLOURS` (`packages/game-logic/src/animals.ts:115`):

| Species | Colour | White text | Dark ink `#3a2e22` |
|---|---|---|---|
| bunny | `#d3d3d3` | **1.50** | 8.80 |
| cat | `#f4a460` | **2.03** | 6.48 |
| parrot | `#2ecc71` | **2.10** | 6.27 |
| snake | `#6b8e23` | **3.81** | 3.46 |
| hedgehog | `#8b7d6b` | **4.00** | 3.29 |
| fox | `#d45500` | **4.12** | 3.20 |
| dog | `#8b6914` | 5.09 ✓ | 2.59 |
| bat | `#4a4a4a` | 8.86 ✓ | 1.49 |

16px bold is not "large text", so the threshold is 4.5:1. Six species fail, and
three fail catastrophically — a white name on a light-grey bunny pill is close to
invisible. The 0.85 alpha over painted room art makes the real numbers worse than
the table.

In a game whose entire emotional proposition is *this animal has a name*, the
name is the wrong thing to lose. And a 7-year-old will not think "poor contrast";
she will think the bunny hasn't got a name yet.

Fix: pick the ink from the pill's luminance rather than hardcoding white —
`L(colour) > 0.45 ? COLOURS.text : '#ffffff'` — which the table above shows gives
every species ≥ 5:1. Raise the pill alpha to 1.0 while you are there.

Same class, same file group:

- Popup close `✕` — `#999` on `#fffaf0` = **2.74:1** (`AnimalDetailsPopup.ts:243`)
- Stat percentages — `#888` on `#fffaf0` = **3.41:1** (`AnimalDetailsPopup.ts:304`)

### 7. Two controls sit on top of the HUD orbs — NEW · high

**Decorate button.** `RoomView.ts:286-289` places a 120×40 rectangle at
`(width − 70, 55)` → x width−130…width−10, y 35…75. The HUD's right-hand cluster
occupies x width−212…width−16 at y 18…62 (`HUDView.ts:161-200`), and the audio
toggle's hit circle is x width−64…width−16, y 16…64. They overlap across
48 × 27 px, and `uiContainer` is added after `gameContainer`, so the HUD draws
over the button and takes the tap.

A child tapping the right-hand half of "Decorate" turns the music off. She has no
model for why. It also sits 10px from the screen edge, inside the 16px
`SAFE_MARGIN`, and at 40px tall it is under `MIN_TAP = 48` with no hit-area floor
— the landscape pass floored `createButton`/`createTextButton`, but this is a raw
`add.rectangle` and was missed.

**Room and corridor titles.** `createPillTitle` at `(width/2, 55)` renders 66px
tall at 28px type (measured: Nunito bold 28px line box = 38px, plus `padY*2`), so
y 22…88 — while the HUD's second row (time-of-day and weather pills) occupies
y 78…106. Measured widths put "Cat Room" at x 306…506 on a phone, straddling both
pills. The bottom ~10px of the room title is drawn across the "Morning" pill.

Fix: give the HUD a declared height and lay every view's title beneath it, from
one shared constant. `LeftRailView.ts:60` already hardcodes `hudH = 110` for
exactly this reason — it should be exported and used, not re-guessed per view.

### 8. The top of "Welcome" opens the wrong thing — NEW · medium-high

`LeftRailView.ts:394-410`. Within a 96px arrival card:

- the Welcome button is centred at `y + 80` and is ~47px tall → y+56…y+103
  (7px past the bottom of its own card)
- the story text (2 lines, 14px) occupies y+30…y+66
- the "tap the card for details" hit rect is `cardH − 30` = 66px tall → y+0…y+66,
  and it is added to the container **after** the button

So the top ~10px of the Welcome button is covered by the details hit rect, which
wins on render order. Tapping the top edge of Welcome opens the info popup
instead of accepting the animal.

Given the history in `.claude/TRAPS.md` — the Welcome button already once looked
dead for an unrelated reason — a second way for Welcome to not-quite-work is
worth closing. And 7–10s aim high on buttons: they tap the label, and the label
is at the top.

Fix: shrink the card's detail hit rect to `cardH - 48`, or make the whole card
open details and move Welcome to a full-width row beneath the card. Also raise
`cardH` to 104 so the button stops overhanging.

### 9. The arrivals count badge is printed on top of its own label — NEW · medium

`LeftRailView.ts:308-332`. The header text is `★ ARRIVALS WAITING`; the badge
circle is placed at a hardcoded `x + 110` with radius 9.

Measured: `★ ARRIVALS WAITING` at Nunito bold 14px is **154.2px** wide. The badge
therefore covers x+101…x+119 — the middle of the word "WAITING" — and the white
count digit is drawn over the letters.

This is the clearest single example of the "overlapping elements" complaint and
it is a two-line fix: measure the label and place the badge at
`label.x + label.width + 10`.

(Related: `★` U+2605 is outside the latin/latin-ext subsets in
`public/fonts/fonts.css`, so the star alone falls back to a system face
mid-label.)

### 10. The primary navigation is four icons from three different sets — NEW · medium

`NavBarView.ts:46-56`. Resolution order, against what is actually on disk:

| Tab | Key looked for | Exists? | What renders | Source size |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Home | `nav-home` | yes | painterly cottage in a cream badge | 256px |
| Care | `nav-care` | yes | painterly bowl + brush in a badge | 256px |
| Walk | `nav-walk` | **no** | falls back to `icon-walk` | 128px |
| Supplies (FAB) | `fab-supplies` | **no** | falls back to `icon-supply-run` | 128px |
| Social | `nav-social` | yes | painterly postbox in a badge | 256px |

`assets/signs/nav-play.png` exists, is 256px, is in the same painterly set — and
is referenced by nothing in `src/`. The Walk tab is looking for a filename that
was never created while the file that was created sits unused.

Rendered consequences, from the assets themselves:

- **`icon-walk.png` is a pale grey paw-print with motion lines on transparency**,
  occupying maybe 40% of its canvas. Drawn at 46px on a bar filled
  `0xffffff` at 0.92, it is very low contrast and visually weightless next to
  three saturated circular badges. It does not read as a button.
- **`icon-supply-run.png` is a flat brown truck on transparency** — no badge, no
  outline — dropped into a white circle with a 3px orange ring. It is the most
  prominent control on the screen and the only one drawn in a different style
  from everything around it.
- **`nav-social` is a red postbox.** For "Social". Children aged 7–10 in 2026
  have, for the most part, never posted a letter; postbox→messages→social is an
  adult's three-step metaphor. The scene it opens is friends and gifts.
- **`nav-home` is a thatched fairy-tale cottage**, but "Home" in this game means
  the rescue centre corridor. The child's own A.R.C. building is drawn elsewhere
  in the game; using it here would be both prettier and truer.
- **`nav-care` puts a food bowl and a grooming brush in one 46px circle.** Two
  objects at that size merge into a beige blob. One object per icon.

Why it matters: at this age icons are read as pictures of things, not as
symbols. A child does not decode "postbox ⇒ messages ⇒ friends"; she sees a red
box. Labels help — and the nav does have them at 15/16px — but the icon is what
she aims at.

Fix, cheapest first:
1. Point `walkKey` at `nav-play` (or rename the file). One line, removes two of
   the five inconsistencies.
2. Draw `fab-supplies` in the painterly badge style to match, or use the existing
   `fab-arc.png`.
3. Re-cut `nav-social` as a wrapped gift or two animals together, and `nav-care`
   as a single food bowl. Grooming has its own flow and its own icon.

### 11. The stat block asks a 7-year-old to read percentages — NEW · medium

`AnimalDetailsPopup.ts:279-310` draws five bars — Hunger, Tired, Happy, Health,
Bond — each with a 14px label, an 8px-tall track and a numeric `47%`.

Three problems, all specific to this age:

1. **Percentages.** Percentage is Year 6 in the England national curriculum
   (age 10–11). For most of the target range `47%` carries no meaning; it is
   decoration that costs 36px of panel width.
2. **Two of the five bars mean the opposite of the other three.** The comment at
   `:271-275` is explicit and thoughtful about it — Hunger and Tired are
   "problem" bars that shrink as you help. But they are drawn in the identical
   visual form as Happy/Health/Bond, which grow as you help. The only cue is
   colour, and the pair carrying the load is red/green — the most common
   colour-vision confusion. A child scanning five identical bars has no way to
   know that a full red bar is bad and a full green bar is good.
3. **The labels are words, when purpose-drawn icons are already shipped.**
   `assets/ui/` contains `ui-hunger-icon.png` (a drumstick),
   `ui-sleep-icon.png` (Zzz), `ui-happy-icon.png` (a smiley),
   `ui-health-icon.png` (a green cross in a heart) and `ui-bond-icon.png` (a pink
   heart) — precisely these five stats, drawn legibly, in the game's style. **All
   21 files in `assets/ui/` are referenced nowhere in `src/`**, while
   `AssetLoader.parseEntry` (`lib/AssetLoader.ts:299`) files them as `essential`,
   so they are downloaded on the loading screen every launch and never drawn.

See the panel redesign below for what to do instead.

### 12. The popup's own buttons are below the project's font floor — NEW · medium

Every `createButton` call in `AnimalDetailsPopup.ts` passes `fontSize: '14px'`
(lines 320, 325, 334, 342, 351, 361, 368, 380, 391, 414, 422). The project's own
constant is `MIN_FONT.button = 18` (`ui/constants.ts:158`), with the comment that
14 is "the smallest allowed text (hints, credits)".

The landscape pass raised 36 sites across 13 files to the 14px floor, but the
harness measures at scene start and never taps an animal, so the busiest panel in
the game kept the smallest type. The same applies to the rail — every label in
`LeftRailView` is 14px — and to `RoomView.ts:293` ("🎀 Decorate", 14px).

Fix: raise action-button labels to 18px and let the panel get wider. It has to
get wider anyway (finding 13).

### 13. The close control is an 11×18px target — NEW · medium

`AnimalDetailsPopup.ts:243-245` makes `✕` interactive with no hit shape, so
Phaser uses the text bounds — roughly 11×18px for an 18px glyph. Against
`MIN_TAP = 48` that is a quarter of the required area, at 2.74:1 contrast, with
no label.

The tap-outside-to-dismiss fallback exists (`:428`), which saves it from being
blocking, but a child who has decided to press the X and misses three times
learns the game is unreliable. `createButton` already floors its hit area at
`MIN_TAP`; this control simply does not use it.

Fix: `scene.add.circle(cx, cy, 24, 0, 0).setInteractive()` behind the glyph, and
darken the glyph to `COLOURS.textLight` (6.33:1).

### 14. The overlay screens drop to 10–13px type — NEW · medium

Measured computed font sizes at 812×375, embedded as the game mounts them:

| Screen | Elements below 14px | Smallest |
|---|---|---|
| `paths.html` | 20 | 12px |
| `friends.html` | 6 | 10px (`← Menu`) |
| `adoption.html` | 5 | 13px |

These use `clamp()`, so the small end appears exactly on the smallest device —
where it is least readable. Among the 12px text on the Paths screen are all the
requirement chips a child needs to understand *why* a path is locked: "Bond 3+",
"Walked 5× (3/5)", "Groomed recently".

Sub-`MIN_TAP` controls on the same screens: the audio button is 36×36 on all
three; `← Back to Luna` is 150×40; `← Menu` is 86×32.

Fix: raise every `clamp()` lower bound to 14px minimum, 16px for anything that is
a sentence, and floor the icon buttons at 48×48. Nothing here needs a redesign —
the layouts have room, especially once they scroll.

### 15. The reading level on Paths is several years too high — NEW · medium

Extracted copy from `paths.html`:

> "Aspire to a path — change your mind any time. Each animal needs different
> things to thrive."

and the three card actions: **"Tap to aspire"**, **"Trait: Alert"**,
**"A role opens"**, **"Bond 5 (3/5)"**.

"Aspire" and "thrive" are secondary-school vocabulary. "Tap to aspire" is a verb
a 7-year-old has never met, used intransitively, as a button label. "Trait:" is a
game-designer's word; "A role opens" is barely parseable as English for this age.

The screen is otherwise beautifully judged — "Forever family", "Back to the
wild", "Find Luna a loving home with a human family" are all exactly right. The
problem is confined to the abstract nouns.

Fix (same meaning, ~7-year-old reading age):

| Now | Instead |
|---|---|
| Aspire to a path — change your mind any time | Pick a future for Luna. You can change your mind later. |
| Each animal needs different things to thrive | Every animal needs something different to be happy. |
| Tap to aspire | Choose this |

## Making the animals the visual focus

Right now the animals lose every contest for screen space. On a landscape iPhone,
of 812 × 375 = 304,500 px²:

| Owner | Area | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Left rail (`LeftRailView`) | 280 × 265 | 24% |
| HUD strip (`HUDView`, two rows to y=106) | 812 × 106 | 28% |
| Nav bar (`NavBarView`) | 464 × 80 | 12% |
| **Overlapping / contested** | — | rail∩nav ≈ 8,500px² |
| Left for painted scene and animals | — | **~45%**, of which the good middle is smaller still |

And the animals that *are* in the clear are then written on top of by their own
name pills and status chips (finding 5).

Five changes, in the order I would make them:

**1. Give the stage a name and reserve it.** One helper, used by all three canvas
views:

```ts
// ui/layout.ts
export const HUD_H = 110;        // already hardcoded in LeftRailView.ts:60
export const NAV_H = 96;         // barH(80) + SAFE_MARGIN, from NavBarView
export function getStageBounds(scene) {
  const { width, height } = scene.scale;
  const railW = railIsOpen(scene) ? RAIL_WIDTH : 0;
  return { x: railW, y: HUD_H, w: width - railW, h: height - HUD_H - NAV_H };
}
```

Then `CorridorView`, `RoomView` and `GardenView` resolve anchor fractions against
`stage`, not `scale`. That alone puts all 36 under-rail anchors back on screen
and stops sprite feet disappearing behind the nav bar.

**2. Close the rail by default.** It is 24% of the screen showing counts a child
does not need continuously. Collapse it to a 56px left-edge tab with the arrivals
count on it; tap to slide it in over the scene, tap the scene to slide it out.
The arrivals it holds are the only urgent thing in it, and a badge communicates
that in 56px.

**3. Undo the 2× and re-scale the callers, then grow the animals deliberately.**
Once `size` means what it says, a corridor arrival at 148px on a 375-tall screen
is 39% of the height — which is roughly right and should be a stated number, not
an accident. Target: **the tapped-animal sprite is never smaller than 140px on
any device, and is the largest single object on screen.**

**4. Move every label out from under the animal, and put a halo behind it.**
Name pill below `sprite.getBounds().bottom + 8`; status chips outside
`bounds.right + 6`; sibling icon outside `bounds.left − 6`. Give the sprite a
soft light ellipse or a 2px cream rim so it separates from painted scenery — the
floor shadow at `CorridorView.ts:336` already does half of this job.

**5. Make the animal the subject of its own popup.** Currently the panel dims the
animal by 32% (`AnimalDetailsPopup.ts:92-94` — the comment says it "keeps tapped
animal visible", but the scrim covers the whole screen and the panel frequently
covers the animal too), and shows no picture of it. See below.

## Per-pet information panel

### What a 7–10 year old actually needs

Watch what the child is deciding. She has tapped an animal, and she wants to know
exactly three things:

1. **Who is this?** Name, what kind of animal, what it looks like.
2. **How is it feeling right now, and is that my fault?** One dominant state, not
   five simultaneous readings.
3. **What can I do about it?** One or two obvious actions, and — when she can't
   do something — *why not*.

Everything else is either for later or for an adult.

### What to cut

| Currently shown | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Five stat bars, all at once | Cut to one "how I'm feeling" line + a needs row |
| `47%` numerals | Cut entirely — percentage is Year 6 |
| Hunger and Tired as inverted bars | Cut the bar form; make them needs, not meters |
| `arrivalStory` in italics, every time | Move to a "My story" flip-side; it is beautiful and it is not a decision input |
| Eight action buttons in one column | Cut to two primary + one "More" |
| `Get a {garment} — weather needs it` | Becomes a weather chip that opens the wardrobe |
| `Heal (Sniffly Nose)` / `Take to Vet (Sniffly Nose)` | The illness name belongs in the state line, not in the button |

### What to add

- **The animal.** A large portrait, drawn from the same texture as the sprite, in
  the panel. The panel is currently the one place in the game that talks about an
  animal without showing it.
- **The five shipped stat icons** (`assets/ui/ui-*-icon.png`) instead of word
  labels. Note `ui-bond-icon.png` has "Bond Lvl: 1" baked into the artwork — it
  needs the caption cropped off before use, or a re-cut.
- **A reason when an action is unavailable.** `canLetOutside` already returns
  `{ ok, reason }` and `AnimalDetailsPopup.ts:108-111` throws the reason away. A
  greyed button that says *why* teaches the game's rules; a button that vanishes
  teaches nothing. This is the single highest-value addition for this age group.
- **A species fact.** `packages/game-logic/src/species-facts.ts` holds
  kid-tested facts with an emoji each, capped at 90 characters, with
  `pickRandomFact()` — currently only used at arrival. "Bunnies do happy jumps
  called binkies when they feel safe" is exactly the reward for opening a panel.

### Proposed layout

Landscape, so use the width. Two columns, ~560 × 300, centred, at a depth above
the rail and nav:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                                          [ X ] │  48px hit
│   ┌──────────────┐    Luna                                     │  24px bold
│   │              │    a tortoiseshell cat                      │  16px
│   │   PORTRAIT   │                                             │
│   │   160×160    │    ┌──────────────────────────────────┐     │
│   │  (live state │    │  🍗  Luna is hungry               │     │  20px, one line
│   │   sprite)    │    └──────────────────────────────────┘     │
│   │              │                                             │
│   └──────────────┘    🍗●●  😴●    😀●●●   ➕●●●   💗●●         │  need dots
│                                                                │
│   💗💗💗♡♡  Best friends: 3 of 5                                │  18px
│                                                                │
│   ┌────────────────┐  ┌────────────────┐  ┌──────────────┐     │
│   │  🍗  Feed her  │  │  🎾  Play      │  │   More…      │     │  56px tall
│   └────────────────┘  └────────────────┘  └──────────────┘     │  18px labels
│                                                                │
│   🐾 Did you know? A group of kittens is called a kindle!      │  16px
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

Design decisions and why:

- **One state line, not five bars.** Derive the single most urgent need
  (`getUrgentNeed` already exists in `packages/game-logic/src/needs.ts` and is
  already used by the HUD and the rail counts) and say it as a sentence with the
  animal's name and the matching icon. `getNeedSpeech` gives the animal's voice
  version — "I'm really hungry…" — which is warmer still and already written.
- **Dots, not bars, not percentages.** Five levels, filled left to right, one
  icon each. A 7-year-old counts dots accurately and cannot read 47%. Crucially
  **dots always mean the same thing** — more dots is more of that thing, and the
  icon says whether that is good — which removes the inverted-bar trap entirely.
  Hunger shows a drumstick with 2 of 5 filled: "a bit hungry". No colour is
  load-bearing.
- **Hearts for bond, with a target.** "3 of 5" is countable; `Bond 47%` is not.
  It also makes the Paths unlock legible in advance: the child can see she needs
  five hearts before anything happens at bond 50.
- **Two primary actions, always in the same two places.** Feed and Play never
  move, so the child builds muscle memory. Everything conditional goes behind
  "More…", which opens a second sheet listing Walk / Groom / Vet / Garden /
  Wardrobe / What happens next — each with a reason line when unavailable
  ("Luna needs to be well before a walk"). This is what removes the variable
  panel height in finding 4 for good: the primary panel is a fixed size.
- **The portrait uses the live visual state.** The same `deriveVisualState`
  the sprite layer uses, so a sick animal looks sick in her own panel. `sick`
  art exists and is already wired (audit follow-up).
- **Fixed 560 × 300** fits inside 812 × 375 with the HUD and nav clear, and
  inside 1024 × 768 with room to grow. No arithmetic height, no overflow.
- **Depth.** Render into a dedicated `popupContainer` at depth 800 — above the
  rail (50) and nav, below the picker (900) — instead of into `gameContainer`.

The story and the full stat detail live on a second face of the same card,
reached by tapping the portrait: "My story" with the arrival text, the species
fact, adoption progress. Optional, so it costs nothing to a child who does not
want it, and rewards the one who does.

earlier being redone.

   `adoption.html`, `friends.html`.

   `e.message`.

Nothing below matters if a child is stuck on one of these screens.

   and replace every hardcoded 40 / 55 / 80 / 110 / `height − 125` in the views.

## Not verified — do not treat as confirmed

Listed so none of it is mistaken for measured fact.

1. **Nothing here was seen rendering in the actual game.** WebGL does not
   initialise in the review harness and the traps warn off click-testing through
   `ErrorOverlay`. The Phaser-side findings are geometry computed from the source
   plus measured font metrics; they have not been photographed. The three HTML
   overlay screens *were* loaded and measured in a real browser at real
   viewports — those numbers are observed.

2. **Input priority in the rail/nav overlap (finding 3d) is reasoned, not
   observed.** Phaser sorts its input list by depth, and the rail is depth 50
   against the nav's 0, so the rail's Welcome button should take the tap. I did
   not confirm which object actually receives the pointer. The *visual* covering
   is certain either way — the rail's paper is 0.96 alpha over the tab.

3. **Line heights are `fontBoundingBox` measurements from Chrome**, not Phaser's
   own `MeasureText` (which pixel-scans). They agree closely in my experience but
   the marginal overlaps — the room title's ~10px intrusion into the HUD pill row
   (finding 7), the corridor title's ~3.5px — could move by a few pixels either
   way. The large overlaps do not depend on this.

4. **Device sizes.** All figures use 812×375 and 1024×768, the project's own
   harness viewports. Newer iPhones are taller in landscape (393pt, 430pt), where
   `paths.html`'s 384px content would fit — though `friends.html` at 414px still
   would not. The failures are worst on the shortest devices, and I have not
   enumerated the fleet.

5. **`renderApprenticeDecorations` and `ApprenticeDecorations.ts` were not
   read.** They draw into the same canvas at the same depth and may add further
   overlaps I have not accounted for.

6. **Scenes not examined:** `WalkScene`, `PlayScene`, `KitchenMinigameScene`,
   `AdoptionMatchScene`, `SocialScene`, `AccountScene`, `SignupScene`,
   `LoginScene`, `VetScene`, `GroomingScene`, `DepotScene`, `SupplyRunScene`.
   Together they are over 8,000 lines. The `* 2` sprite consequence (finding 5)
   very likely reaches `WalkScene` and `PlayScene`, which pass explicit sizes,
   but I did not check where their labels sit.

7. **`cockpit.html` was not reviewed** — it is a driving mockup per the brief. For
   the record it carries 16 `font-size` declarations below 14px, three of them at
   9px, which matters if any of it graduates into the game.

8. **`friends.html`'s mount path was not traced.** It is not in
   `InGameOverlay.PAGE_URLS`; I measured it standalone with `?embed=1` on the
   assumption it is mounted the same way. If it is reached some other way, its
   `overflow-y: hidden` clipping at 414/375 may or may not apply.

9. **I did not check whether `room-anchors.json` loads successfully at runtime.**
   The 36%-under-rail figure assumes `RoomAnchors` resolves those anchors; if the
   fetch fails the procedural fallbacks apply, which are also partly under the
   rail (finding 3b) but in different places.

10. **Contrast figures are computed against the flat species colour**, not against
    the painted room background the pill actually sits on at 0.85 alpha. The real
    ratios will be somewhat worse than the table, not better.
