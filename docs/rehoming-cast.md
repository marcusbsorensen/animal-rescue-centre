# Rehoming Cast — the humans who give our animals homes

**Status:** v0.1 draft for review. Edit freely; art-generation prompts will reference this as source of truth once frozen.

## Design principles

1. **Integrated, not flagged.** A character's gender identity, disability, body size or ethnicity is never the *reason* they're on screen. They're a person with a life who happens to be ready to love an animal. The game never says "meet Nova, they're non-binary!" — Nova is just Nova. Their pronouns show up in the copy as naturally as anyone else's.

2. **Recognisable across visits.** 17 characters, each with a strong visual signature (hair, glasses, a favourite colour, a mobility aid, a signature bag). Kids should go "oh, the beekeeper's back!" before they read the name.

3. **A matched pair of traits.** Every household carries 2-3 *lifestyle traits* that gate which animals fit. The bond-prep work the player does is what turns a mismatch into a match over time.

4. **Return-visit delight.** Adopters don't vanish after collecting a pet. They come back to visit, to donate, to adopt again, to share photos from the wild. Five return rhythms described below.

5. **Storybook-warm (Aardman / Raymond Briggs chibi).** Soft rounded shapes, gentle brush outlines, warm saturated palette, hand-painted textures. Same illustration treatment as the animals so the world feels one piece.

## Volunteer apprentices (growth system)

Some cast members are too fond of animals to adopt just one. Their defining trait is **"wants to adopt ALL the animals every visit"** — a loving impulse that has to be channelled, not just reined in. These characters can be **recruited as volunteer apprentices** at A.R.C., helping with care tasks, greeting new arrivals, feeding runs, and cleaning. Recruiting apprentices is what unlocks **new species**, **expanded rooms**, and **more concurrent rescues** — the rescue centre literally grows because more hands want to help.

| Apprentice | From | Why them | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rhubarb** | Two-Houses household #30 | Wants to adopt every single animal; skater-rocker energy directed into care work; splits the week so can volunteer both weekends and a weekday | First apprentice slot — unlocks the **apprentices room** and one extra daily care task |
| **Amara** | Kumar-Ishii family #13 | 9yo tomboy, already grew up with an ARC cat, mum is a vet; climbs every tree at the centre looking for cat-friendly perches | Unlocks **second concurrent rescue slot** for cats |
| **Kofi** | Grandpa Theo's grandkids #14 | 10yo weekend visitor with his grandpa; reads every animal care book in the library | Unlocks **new species slot** (his request determines which — parrot or snake typically) |

Apprentices don't replace staff — they shadow the player. In gameplay terms they bring small bonuses (faster feeding, gentler arrival calming, occasional found-items during cleaning).

## Lifestyle traits (the matching grammar)

Each household carries 2-3 of these. Animals need certain traits in their home for a *good match*. Bond-prep gameplay teaches the player what each animal needs.

- **Active / outdoors** — lots of walks, space, running
- **Quiet / patient** — routines, calm energy, no kids' chaos
- **Cozy / lap-focused** — small space, lap time, gentle spoiling
- **Kids / family** — tolerates noise, enjoys play, won't snap
- **Experienced handler** — handles shy/anxious rescues, medical care
- **First-time** — needs an easy-mode animal
- **Garden / rural** — can roam, forage, be outside
- **Flat / urban** — small & adaptable
- **Creative chaos** — paint, projects, messy love
- **Specific-interest** — autistic focus on snakes, reptile expert, etc.

## Return-visit rhythms (the community loop)

1. **Just-dropped-by** — waves from the garden, brief "look how Sprout's grown!" scene. Passive delight.
2. **Donation run** — brings toys / food / money. Refills Depot stocks. Occasionally rare items.
3. **Second adoption** — returning family looking for another animal. Matches get a "trusted adopter" boost (easier criteria, faster approval).
4. **Photo letter** — a painted postcard arrives from the family, shows the pet in their new home / a different season.
5. **Wild-visit invite** — (only from rewilders like Benji) unlocks a visit-in-the-wild scene where the player drives out to see the animal in its habitat, and the animal recognizes them.

## Art-generation notes

Once the roster is frozen, the batch plan:

- **~28 households × 3 states each** (~40 individual faces including kids + housemates) = ~120 sprites:
  - **Neutral portrait** (catalogue / adopter list)
  - **Greeting** (arms-out-happy, meeting a new animal)
  - **Departing with pet** (walking away, pet on lead / in carrier)
- Plus ~12 **return-visit variants** (donation bag, postcard in hand, wild-visit outdoor shot)
- Total: ~84 sprites, 512×512 PNG transparent, painted chibi style matching animals.

All generated via the GPT-Image-1.5 pipeline with 2-3 reference images of the painted animals to lock the art style. Same regen script family as `tools/regen-play-poses.sh`.

## Decisions (locked 2026-04-23)

- **Dynamic matching.** The game picks which adopter shows up for a given animal based on trait fit from the pool of 17 households. No hand-authored pairings — every match is earned by the player preparing the animal appropriately.
- **Visitors can become adopters.** A character who starts as a casual visitor / donor (e.g. Theo brings his grandkids to visit, Khan drops off donations) can *evolve* into an adopter over time if the player keeps engaging them warmly. This creates a slow-burn relationship arc — kids learn that trust is built gradually.
- **Rewilded animals may return to visit — but stay outdoors.** A rewilded animal accompanying its rewilder (e.g. Benji bringing his fox back) plays only in the garden / outdoor zones. They don't come back into the rescue rooms. Reinforces "they live free now" while still allowing the player to see them and feel the bond.
