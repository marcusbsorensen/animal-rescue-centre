# UI audit — what stops A.R.C. looking professional

**Written 2026-08-31**, at 874x402 (iPhone 17 Pro landscape, the
Capacitor shell's real viewport). Ranked, so it can be picked from.

## Coverage, and what it is not

Looked at by eye: welcome, login, intro, corridor, kitchen, garden, plus
the Care view and the corridor under the side-rail prototype. Menu and
the species room failed to capture (the harness did not set the state
they need) and are unaudited. **Not** covered: the fourteen in-game
overlay screens, WalkScene, DepotScene, PtvDrive, Social, Account. The
findings below are systematic rather than per-screen, so they very
probably hold there too — but that is inference, not observation.

The counts are exact. The judgements are mine.

---

## 1. There are three visual languages, and no rule for which to use

The single biggest thing. Everything below is a symptom of it.

| Language | Where | Looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Painted wood | the 21 sign screens, view titles | hand-painted boards, bolts, grain, warm |
| Translucent glass | HUD pills, nav bar, kitchen panels | flat white, 92% alpha, drop shadow — generic mobile game |
| Flat vector / emoji | rail paw prints, zone arrows, bullet dots, icon fallbacks | neither of the above |

The kitchen screenshot has **all three in one frame**: a gold painted
title pill, a white glass "Everyone is well-fed!" panel, white glass
HUD pills, a flat green button, and flat paw glyphs in the rail.

A child crosses from a hand-painted signpost yard into a screen of
translucent white pills every single session. That gap does more damage
to "professional" than any individual screen's layout, because it reads
as two different products stitched together.

**This is a decision, not a bug.** Either the painted language wins and
the chrome gets re-drawn to match, or the flat language wins and the
signs get simplified. Both are defensible. What cannot work is the
current position, which is neither.

## 2. Thirty-nine font stacks

Counted: **500 `font-family` declarations across the sign screens,
resolving to 39 distinct stacks.** The game side declares three
(`FONTS.title`, `.body`, `.chalk` in `ui/constants.ts`).

The DOM screens reach for Barlow Condensed, Inter, DM Sans, Oswald,
Kalam, Chalkboard SE, Fredoka, Caveat and more. Only Fredoka and Caveat
are known to the game side at all.

The visible symptom is on the login board right now: **"TYPE YOUR NAME"
renders in system sans** while everything around it is the rounded game
face. With 39 stacks and web fonts that may not all have loaded, some
fallback was always going to fire somewhere.

Fixable mechanically, and the fix is the same shape as the colour one
below: one type scale, declared once, used by both sides.

## 3. Text painted straight onto painted art

**Readability, not taste — this one hurts a child more than it hurts the
brand.**

The garden's empty state — "No pets yet!" and the two lines explaining
bonding — is set in mid-grey directly over painted grass, flowers and
dappled light. It is close to illegible. Everything else in the game
that carries words sits on a plate: a board, a pill, a panel. This
does not.

The rule the sign screens already follow (`_signpost-physics.css`:
nothing on screen that could not be a sketch of something real) has a
natural counterpart here: **no words without something to be written
on.**

## 4. 610 raw colour literals against 276 token uses

`COLOURS` in `ui/constants.ts` is a proper palette — brand greens pulled
from the A.R.C. lettering, reds from the heart, a documented rationale
per family. It is bypassed roughly **69% of the time**: 610 raw
`0xRRGGBB` literals across 45 files, against 276 uses of the token.

That is why near-identical greens and browns drift apart across screens.
The palette does not need designing; it needs using.

## 5. Controls at or past the screen edge

The garden's zone arrows: the left one is partly behind the arrivals
rail, the right one is flush to the screen edge. Both are unlabelled
white circles.

`.claude/TRAPS.md` already records two cousins of this found by
measurement — the garden's upgrade button under the nav bar, and its
left zone arrow inside the rail's reserved column — and notes both had
been unreachable on every viewport for as long as they had existed. So
this is a recurring class, not an incident. The play area exists to stop
it; the garden does not fully use it.

## 6. The kitchen ignores the play area

`KitchenView.ts:71` draws its background at full `width`, where the
corridor, room and garden draw to `play.w`. So the kitchen art runs
under the arrivals rail and behind the Dynamic Island. Visible in the
capture as art continuing past the rail's left edge.

Already listed as open work in `landscape-relayout-2026-08-31.md`.

## 7. Emoji standing in for commissioned art

Painterly 256px icons were commissioned for the nav in August. Beside
them sit emoji fallbacks — a literal box for the Supplies FAB when the
texture is missing — and flat glyph paw prints in the arrivals rail.
`AVATAR_EMOJIS` is fine and deliberate: those are the child's own
avatar choices. The furniture ones are not.

## Ranked

By effect on "looks like a finished product":

1. **Pick one visual language** (§1) — structural; everything else is
   downstream of it, and doing the others first means doing them twice.
2. **One type scale, shared** (§2) — mechanical, highly visible, and
   fixes the system-sans button on the way past.
3. **Plates under all text** (§3) — smallest job on this list, and the
   only one a child is currently losing information to.
4. **Use the palette** (§4) — mechanical, invisible individually,
   compounding across screens.
5. **Sweep the edges** (§5) — measurable, and the harness can hold it.
6. **Kitchen play area** (§6) — one line.
7. **Retire the emoji furniture** (§7) — needs art for anything missing.

If only one thing gets done: **§1**, because the other six are cheaper
and better-aimed once it is settled.

If only one *cheap* thing gets done: **§3**, then §6 — an afternoon
between them, and the garden stops hiding its own instructions.
